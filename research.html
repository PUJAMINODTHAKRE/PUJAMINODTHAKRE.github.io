Knowledge Representation for Healthcare Systems

A comparative evaluation of knowledge-representation paradigms for healthcare question answering, using a common cardiovascular knowledge setting and a benchmark designed to increase reasoning complexity.

Research question

How do different representation paradigms compare when a system must answer healthcare questions that move from simple conditions toward multi-relational and contextual queries?

Benchmark & evaluation

The study uses 47 anonymized cardiovascular patient records and five benchmark queries. Evaluation considers query coverage, reasoning depth and explainability.

Key finding

The published benchmark reports complete query coverage for the Knowledge Graph representation, including multi-hop and contextual reasoning with path-based explainability.

How the representations were aligned

The work built a common schema around the cardiovascular domain so that the paradigms could be compared within a shared information setting. Implementations included Python-based logic and rules, relational queries, frame-style slot–filler structures, RDF/OWL modelling and Knowledge Graph queries through SPARQL.

Why the comparison is useful

Query coverage gives a direct answer to whether the benchmark can be answered at all; reasoning depth describes the number of inferential steps involved; explainability records how the answer can be traced or justified. Together these dimensions make the comparison more interpretable than a single performance number.

Scope and limitations

The study is a comparative benchmark rather than a clinical deployment. It uses 47 records, includes manually curated relationships, and does not claim that a particular representation should be adopted universally for healthcare systems.

Ongoing research · 2025–Present

Cryptocurrency Markets & Geopolitical Risk

A high-frequency event-study project examining the response of cryptocurrency and traditional financial markets to geopolitical shocks during the Israel–Gaza conflict.

Research question

How do time-stamped geopolitical information and changing war-risk signals coincide with short-horizon movements in financial markets?

Research design

The project integrates time-stamped geopolitical news with hourly financial observations and uses an Hourly War Risk Index (HWRI) to distinguish escalation and de-escalation shocks.

Reported result

In the current study, responses are heterogeneous and short-lived across assets, with no persistent market-wide relationship between war risk and abnormal returns over the full study period.

Research direction

The broader direction is to connect structured information with statistical learning: representing domain context explicitly, extracting usable signals from language and data, and evaluating how those signals affect model outputs or observed behaviour.
